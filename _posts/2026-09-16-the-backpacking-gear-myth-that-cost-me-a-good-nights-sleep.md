Let's begin with a big misconception: that getting the right backpack is the most important piece of gear. It seems like common sense to want a fancy, comfortable pack. It is important, but I'd side with most backpacking enthusiasts and say the most important item to start with is your sleeping system, followed by your tent, and third your backpack. I learned this lesson the hard way: even after I listened to the experts, I thought I knew better and bought a really fancy backpack and tent before finally investing in a nice sleeping bag. Turns out, carrying an uncomfortable backpack is something you can deal with, but having poor sleep on the trail makes for a miserable journey.



Starting with the sleep system — what is it? It's actually three items that generally make up the system. The bottom layer is usually a closed-cell foam mat, which blocks the ground from stealing your heat at night. The middle layer is a sleeping pad — these can be inflated to give you a more comfortable experience, like a little air mattress. The closed-cell foam mat and sleeping pad can be used together or separately, but a sleeping pad alone makes for a more comfortable sleep than a closed-cell foam mat alone. Third, and most important, is your sleeping bag.



Each item has its own R-value, which rates its insulating properties, and this can vary by piece of gear. The higher the R-value, the better the insulation.



There are pros and cons to a higher or lower R-value. A higher value means you'll lose less heat at night but will weigh more, whereas a lower R-value will be lighter but can't be used in low temps.



On a sleeping bag, there are usually two temperature ratings: a comfort rating and a minimum rating. The comfort rating refers to the lowest temperature you can sleep in comfortably, and the minimum rating is the lowest temperature your bag will keep you alive in.



The R-value and temperature rating you need will vary depending on the season and conditions you'll be hiking in.
